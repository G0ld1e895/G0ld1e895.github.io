Introduction

The nation's cuisine is a fusion of African, Indian, Indigenous , European, Chinese & Middle Eastern infuences creating a distinct Trinbagonian identity that reflects the country's multicultural history. Food culture in Trinidad and Tobago serves as a primary means for bringing people together where meals are shared amongst family and friends, especially as a traditional Sunday lunch and festive occasions like Christmas where foods such as callaloo and crab or pelau enjoyed together. Street foods like doubles are deliciously satisfying for persons who are busy on mornings and as a post-Carnival staple.

My Favourite Foods

  • Doubles - I enjoy this spicy and savoury edible as an appetizer before breakfast.
  • Callaloo - For me,callaloo is best eaten with rice and peas and stewed chicken during lunch time.
  • Dhalpuri - For me,the best savoury roti, filled with seasoned, spicy ground dhal compliments any spicy, curried meat such as chicken or duck and is suitable for lunch or dinner with family or friends.
  • Pelau - I enjoy this meal with "a portion of vegetable salad" for Sunday lunch with family.

Favourite Local Foods

Food Description Image
Doubles Iconic street food made of 2 pieces of soft, fried, flat bread called bara filled with spiced, curried chickpeas. A vegan dish served with a variety of condiments such as tamarind sauce, kuchela and cucumber.
Callaloo Thick, creamy, stew-like dish made with dasheen bush, simmered with okra,coconut milk and a variety of aromatics including pumpkin, carrots, scallions, garlic and pimento peppers.
Dhalpuri A flatbread stuffed with a seasoned mixture of ground split peas(spiced with turmeric, cumin, garlic and cilantro locally called shadow beni).
Pelau One-pot rice dish with chicken browned in caramelized sugar with rice, pigeon peas, coconut milk, various vegetables and fresh herbs such as coriander or shadow beni.
